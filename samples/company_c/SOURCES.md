# Public figures behind the ClearClaim AI sample

ClearClaim AI is a fictional company (`SYNTHETIC SAMPLE`). Its deck makes claims about the real US
dental market so the market and competitor research can check them against public sources. This
file records which public figures the planted claims are meant to be far from. Checked 2026-11-07.

| Deck claim | Public figure | Source | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| "There are 400,000 independent dental practices in the United States." | "As of 2025, there are 205,088 professionally active dentists in the U.S." A practice has at least one dentist, so there are fewer practices than dentists. | ADA Health Policy Institute, The dentist workforce: https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dentist-workforce | At least 1.95x, above the 1.5x "possibly overstated" threshold |
| "40% of dental insurance claims are denied on first submission." | About 15-20%, for example "19.3% of claims denied on first submission". | Vendor blog, Clerri: https://clerri.com/blog/dental-insurance-claim-denial-statistics/ (no primary study found; see note) | About 2x |
| $420,000 operating costs for 14 full-time employees ($30,000 each) | Private industry, Service occupations (the lowest-cost occupation group): total compensation $23.39 per hour worked, Table 4. x 2,080 hours = $48,651 a year, the floor in code. | BLS, Employer Costs for Employee Compensation, December 2025 (released 2026-03-20), Table 4: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ecec_03202026.htm | $30,000 is below the $48,651 floor |
| "No other company offers AI agents for dental insurance claims." | Several companies sell AI insurance verification or claims automation for dental practices, for example Toothy AI (Y Combinator W25) and Overjet (AI Insurance Verification, June 2025). | https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/toothy-ai ; https://www.healthcaredive.com/press-release/20250603-overjet-launches-ai-powered-dental-insurance-verification | The claim is contradicted |

Note on the denial rate: no primary study (ADA, a payer report or a peer-reviewed paper) with a
national first-submission denial rate for dental claims was found. Vendor pages cite 15-20%. The
deck's 40% is about twice the highest vendor figure, so the gap holds with any of them, but the
research step should report the source type.

The earlier deck (September 2025) says 250,000 practices and a $900M market. 250,000 x $3,600 =
$900M, so that deck is internally consistent; only the change to 400,000 and $12B is planted.
